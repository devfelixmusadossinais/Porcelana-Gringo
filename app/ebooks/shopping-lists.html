Minimum tools
  • 1 small glass or ceramic bowl
  • Silicone spoon or spatula
  • 1 small glass container with a lid
  • Fine-mesh strainer
  • Small saucepan for a double boiler
  • Cotton rounds
Probably at home
  • Filtered waterUsed in many recipes. Use drinking-quality water; filtering doesn't make water sterile.
  • Your usual gentle facial cleanserUse as directed, and keep cleansing gentle if your skin feels dry or sensitive.
  • Facial sunscreenApply broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher every morning, including to your neck and chest. Choose water resistance for outdoor use and reapply according to the label.
Patch test first

Test each new ingredient and the finished mixture on a small area of your inner forearm. For a rinse-off recipe, rinse after its intended contact time, then watch the area for at least 24 hours. Don't use it if you notice itching, burning, redness, or swelling. A clear patch test doesn't rule out a later reaction. If you have sensitive skin, a skin condition, or are pregnant, check with a dermatologist before starting.

Reading labels · 1 of 2

What to Look For on the Label

Simple choices for your shopping cart.

Fragrance-free aloe vera gel
Drugstore or beauty aisle

Look for: A commercially preserved gel intended for skin, with aloe listed prominently. Follow its directions and storage instructions.

Avoid: Added fragrance, dyes, and drying alcohol such as alcohol denat. Preservatives help protect the packaged product; don't avoid them simply to get a shorter ingredient list. Skip fresh aloe leaves and yellow aloe sap. Once mixed with other ingredients, the gel's original shelf life no longer applies.

Sweet almond oil
Drugstore or beauty aisle

Look for: Pure sweet almond oil intended for skin, preferably in a small, dark bottle.

Avoid: Fragranced blends and bitter almond oil. Close tightly and store away from heat and light. If you have an almond or tree nut allergy, don't use it; ask your dermatologist before choosing a substitute.

Shea butter
Drugstore or beauty aisle

Look for: Pure shea butter without added fragrance.

Avoid: Substituting a lotion that only contains shea butter; it won't behave like pure butter. Natural color and scent can vary. Melt gently in a double boiler and let it cool before applying.

Rosewater without added fragrance
Drugstore or beauty aisle

Look for: A rose hydrosol intended for facial use, without added perfume or drying alcohol. Check the storage directions.

Avoid: Room sprays, fragrance waters, and rose essential oil. Rosewater's natural aromatic components may still irritate sensitive skin. Diluted Rose Dew Mist has no established shelf life; make it fresh and discard leftovers.

Rosehip seed oil (optional)
Drugstore or beauty aisle

Look for: Pure rosehip seed oil in a small, dark bottle, labeled for skin use.

Avoid: Large bottles you won't finish within the label's recommended period. Discard if it smells rancid or changes noticeably. The plan uses it at night; apply broad-spectrum SPF 30+ every morning regardless of which oil you use.

Coconut oil and cosmetic-grade beeswax
Grocery store or lip balm supply section

Look for: Virgin coconut oil without added fragrance. Choose cosmetic-grade beeswax pellets or a suitable plant-based wax labeled for lip balm use.

Avoid: Candle wax and scented craft wax. Plant waxes aren't always interchangeable with beeswax in equal amounts; follow the supplier's formulation guidance or skip that substitution. Coconut oil naturally firms up when cool and softens when warm. The recipe lists 1 tsp of wax.

Cotton rounds
Drugstore or grocery store

Look for: Soft, fragrance-free cotton rounds.

Avoid: Rough rounds or ones that shed fibers. The plan estimates about 2 per day, plus 2 large rounds for Cotton Glow Masks. Keep fibers and homemade mixtures away from your eyes.

Reading labels · 2 of 2

What to Look For on the Label

Simple choices for your shopping cart.

Pure honey
Grocery store

Look for: Honey listed as the only ingredient, in a sealed food-grade container.

Avoid: Honey-flavored syrups and blends with added sugar. Crystallization is normal. Don't use honey if you're allergic to it, and don't apply food-based masks to broken or irritated skin.

Rice flour and oats
Grocery store

Look for: Plain rice flour and unflavored rolled oats without added sugar. Grind oats into a fine powder before use.

Avoid: Baking mixes and flavored oatmeal. Don't scrub with rice flour or oats; use a light touch and rinse gently. Store dry ingredients tightly closed. Skip oat recipes if you're allergic to oats.

Yogurt and milk
Grocery store

Look for: Plain whole-milk yogurt without sugar, flavoring, or sweeteners. Choose pasteurized milk or an unsweetened plant-based milk you tolerate.

Avoid: Expired products and dairy ingredients if you have a milk allergy. Lactose-free dairy still contains milk proteins. For Oat Cloud Mask, choose a plant-based milk that fits your allergies.

Green tea and chamomile
Grocery store

Look for: Plain tea bags or loose leaves without added flavors, fruit, or essential oils.

Avoid: Very strong brews, which may feel drying. Cool tea completely before skin contact. If you're allergic to chamomile or related plants, skip it; use green tea only if you know you tolerate it. Keep tea and tea bags away from the eyes.

Whole flaxseed
Grocery store

Look for: Whole seeds, rather than ground flaxseed, in a sealed package.

Avoid: Saving homemade gel for another day. Make it fresh, let it cool, and discard leftovers; refrigeration doesn't establish a safe shelf life. Store the seeds according to the package.

Fruit and vegetables
Grocery store

Look for: Ripe avocado and banana, firm cucumber and carrot, and fresh watermelon without bruised or spoiled areas. For Pumpkin Comfort Mask, use plain canned pumpkin.

Avoid: Spoiled produce, pumpkin pie filling, and added spices. Wash produce thoroughly. Patch test every finished mixture; foods can irritate skin or trigger allergies. Keep contact brief and rinse immediately if uncomfortable.

Small containers and spray bottle
Home

Look for: Containers with secure lids and a spray bottle that can be thoroughly cleaned according to its manufacturer's directions.

Avoid: Containers with lingering odors, cracks, or residue. Wash thoroughly and dry completely. Use heat only if the material is rated for it; cleaning doesn't sterilize homemade preparations.

Storage & freshness

Where to keep it

Rice Glow Water

Use a clean container during preparation. Don't ferment it or save it overnight. If you refrigerate it briefly to cool, that doesn't establish a safe shelf life.

Green Tea Comfort Toner

Cool in a clean, covered container. Don't rely on the original 48-hour storage estimate; the mixture hasn't been preservation-tested.

Rose Dew Mist

Use a clean, dry spray bottle. Don't assume the diluted mixture will keep for 3 days in the refrigerator.

Flaxseed gel and Silky Feel Serum

Use clean containers during preparation. Don't save the gel overnight; its original 24-hour storage estimate wasn't validated.

Silky Lip Balm

Keep in a clean, dry container and use a clean spatula. Keep water, honey, and sugar out of the balm container. Don't assume a 30-day shelf life.

Shea Comfort Cream

Keep in a clean, dry glass container and use a clean spatula. Don't add water or aloe, and don't assume a 30-day shelf life.

Neck & Chest Comfort Cream

Keep in a clean, dry container, away from heat and light. Don't add water or assume a 30-day shelf life.

Pure oils for Silky Touch Oil and Evening Comfort Oil

Keep original bottles tightly closed, away from heat and light. Don't return a mixed portion to either bottle.

Masks, Soft Glow Gel, and Eye-Area Comfort Gel

Don't store mixed leftovers. Rinse off food-based masks; don't leave them on overnight. Keep homemade mixtures off eyelids and away from lash lines and eyes.

Keeping what you bought

Oils, shea butter, and wax: keep tightly closed and follow the label's storage directions.

Rosehip seed oil: write the opening date on the bottle and follow its after-opening instructions.

Packaged aloe gel, yogurt, and milk: follow the label after opening. Those instructions don't apply once ingredients are mixed.

Oats, rice flour, and flaxseed: keep tightly closed and store as directed on the package.

When to discard

Discard rice water if it smells sour. Don't ferment it for facial use.

Discard flaxseed gel or serum if its smell, color, or texture changes.

Discard rosehip seed oil if it smells rancid or changes noticeably.

Discard leftover water-based mixtures after use. Contamination may be present even when a mixture looks and smells normal.

Swaps & skin comfort

Allergies & care

Oats: if you're allergic, skip Oat Cloud Mask and Rice & Oat Softness Mask.

Milk and yogurt: if you have a milk allergy, skip yogurt masks and dairy substitutions. Lactose-free dairy isn't a substitute for a milk allergy.

Honey: choose pure honey and skip it if you're allergic.

Chamomile: skip it if you're allergic to chamomile or related plants. Use green tea only if you tolerate it.

Fruit, avocado, and pumpkin: patch test the finished mixture, keep contact brief, and rinse immediately if uncomfortable. Don't use ingredients you're allergic to.

Almonds: if you have an almond or tree nut allergy, don't use sweet almond oil. Ask your dermatologist before substituting another oil.

Can't find an ingredient? Skip that recipe and continue with the steps that suit you. Patch test every new ingredient and finished mixture for at least 24 hours before facial use. If irritation occurs, rinse off and stop using it. Check with a dermatologist before starting if you have sensitive skin, a skin condition, or are pregnant. Keep homemade mixtures off broken skin, eyelids, lash lines, and eyes.
